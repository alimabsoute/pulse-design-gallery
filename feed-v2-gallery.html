News Feed Concepts

Four redesigned feed layouts — dense, information-rich, alive. Every card capped at 130px. No wasted bars, no empty space. Use these to pick the direction before engineering begins.

Design Concept NF1

The Ticker Board

A scrolling ticker strip brings the sensation of live data without a live backend. Below it, a stat bar gives instant pulse metrics, and a dense 3-column card grid shows all articles at maximum density.

Full Preview — NF1

Pros
  • Ticker conveys live sensation without real-time infrastructure
  • 3-column grid maximizes article density per viewport
  • Stat bar gives instant pulse metrics above the fold
  • Hover-pause on ticker gives readers time to read
Cons
  • Ticker and grid can feel visually disconnected
  • Mobile collapses to 1 column — ticker dominates small screens
  • Signal variety in ticker requires careful ordering logic
Design Concept NF2

The Stream

Single-column terminal-style feed. Every row is exactly 40px — time, signal chip, source, neighborhood, headline, and sentiment bar all on one line. A simulated "NEW" row slides in every 8 seconds.

Full Preview — NF2

Pros
  • Maximum density — 12 articles visible without scrolling on large screens
  • Terminal / Bloomberg aesthetic feels professional and fast
  • Time dividers create clear temporal context
  • "NEW" animation simulates real-time without live data
Cons
  • 1-line headline truncation loses meaningful context
  • No visual hierarchy — all rows feel equally weighted
  • Source column too narrow for longer outlet names
Design Concept NF3

The Trading Floor

Five independent kanban columns — Openings, Closures, Awards, Opinions, Trends. Operators immediately jump to what matters. Self-filtering without a single click.

Full Preview — NF3
Today · 12 articles · 5 open · 1 close · 2 awards · 2 opinions · 2 trends
Pros
  • Zero-click self-filtering — operators go straight to Openings
  • Signal distribution instantly visible from column heights
  • Cards per column are tight — no empty white space
  • Closes column tinted red creates immediate visual alarm
Cons
  • 5 columns very wide — requires horizontal scroll on most screens
  • Low-volume columns (Closures) look sparse and unbalanced
  • Can't easily sort across signal types
Design Concept NF4

Stat Strip + Masonry Grid

Four bold stat cards anchor the top for instant orientation. Below, a two-column asymmetric grid puts high-priority Openings and Awards in the left column, everything else right — editorial hierarchy without a filter bar.

Pros
  • Stat strip gives instant city-level orientation
  • Left-column priority gives editorial hierarchy without UI chrome
  • Cards have room for 1-line excerpt — more context than NF2
  • Sentiment bar on every card makes quality scannable
Cons
  • Left-vs-right logic must be maintained as data grows
  • Stat strip requires a real data layer to stay accurate
  • Two columns collapse awkwardly at narrow widths
