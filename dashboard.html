Strengths

  • Every section gets full width — nothing feels cramped or competing for space.
  • First-time visitors understand the page immediately — graph → trend → rankings.
  • Mobile-friendly: sections stack naturally, city toggle is always accessible.

Watch-outs

  • No persistent element keeps restaurant owners anchored — they may leave after the graph.
D2

Graph Dominant

The node graph owns most of the page. Flash cards float as badges inside the graph zone. The chart is a slim strip. The leaderboard is a horizontal scrolling row of compact tiles — lighter, faster, scroll-forward.

Strengths

  • The graph dominates — this is the WOW moment, maxed out.
  • Flash cards inside the graph feel organic, not bolted-on.
  • Horizontal leaderboard is fast to scan and scroll-native on mobile.
  • Most shareable design — the full-screen graph screenshot is the viral moment.

Watch-outs

  • Horizontal leaderboard loses some data density vs. a full table — rank change and signal hints don't fit well.
  • Flash cards can feel hidden in the corner of the graph for users who don't explore.
  • The slim chart loses the "event annotation" feature that makes the chart fresh daily.

Strengths

  • The chart with event pins is the most data-rich version — and it's the first thing you see.
  • KPI row immediately orients the visitor before they look at anything else.
  • Best for repeat users who come to check the city's momentum score.
  • The event annotations make every day's chart genuinely different from yesterday's.

Watch-outs

  • The node graph below the chart may feel secondary — loses some of its WOW impact.
  • Data-forward first impression could feel cold for casual foodie visitors.
  • More cognitive load above the fold — four KPIs + big chart before anything visual.
D4

Leaderboard Hero

Restaurant owners come for the rankings. The leaderboard dominates the top half — more rows, more data per row, the paid CTA on every line. Flash cards are a sticky strip at the very top. Node graph is compact right-rail. Chart anchors the bottom.

Strengths

  • Restaurant owners see the leaderboard immediately — this is designed for them.
  • The "Unlock full breakdown" CTA on every row is the most aggressive paid conversion path.
  • Flash strip at top ensures the insight cards are never missed, even by quick visitors.
  • More leaderboard rows visible above the fold than any other layout.

Watch-outs

  • Foodie consumers (the larger audience) may not feel spoken to — heavy data focus.
  • The node graph feels demoted to a sidebar widget rather than a centrepiece.
  • Less visually dramatic — the page feels more like a SaaS dashboard than a food product.
D5

Split Screen

Left half is the node graph — persistent, full height, always visible. Right half scrolls independently with chart at top, leaderboard in middle, flash cards at bottom. Desktop-forward. The graph never leaves your sight.

Strengths

  • The graph never leaves your sight — it's a persistent live map while you browse the data.
  • Clicking a node on the left can filter the leaderboard on the right (great interaction to build).
  • Both audiences are served simultaneously — graph for foodies, leaderboard for owners.
  • Feels like a proper intelligence product — not a blog with charts.

Watch-outs

  • Completely breaks on mobile — requires a fundamentally different mobile layout.
  • The right panel feels cramped — chart, leaderboard, and cards compete for height.
  • More complex to build correctly than any other layout.
